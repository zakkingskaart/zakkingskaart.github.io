Settlement bolts are measuring points in façades whose height the city measures periodically. The rate for recent years is calculated from measurements at least 4 years apart. A settlement bolt says something about that one façade; this page only shows the measured rates. The median is the middle value: half sink faster, half slower.

Subsidence measured from space

A satellite measures ground, streets and buildings together; for each block of 100 metres that is an average. A house on piles can stand still while the street in front of it sinks, and vice versa. Only where something reflects the radar signal well (roofs, roads) is there a measurement; not in meadows or on water. Source: Copernicus, the EU's Earth observation programme (Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, European Ground Motion Service (EGMS); bevat aangepaste Copernicus Sentinel-data).
