Rijkswaterstaat benchmarks

There are 111 Rijkswaterstaat benchmarks in Bernheze. For 53 of them we know how fast they have been sinking in recent years; the median is 0.36 mm per year.

Classification: KCAF guideline on foundations under buildings (table 3), the same as the City of Amsterdam's. It describes how fast a measuring point is sinking, not the condition of the foundation.

A benchmark (NAP benchmark) is a small metal bolt in a house, bridge or other structure. Rijkswaterstaat measures its height once every 5 to 15 years. The rate for recent years is calculated from measurements at least 8 years apart. Benchmarks that have not been measured for more than 20 years, or whose measurements show an unexplained jump, count as ‘no recent or too few measurements’. A benchmark moves with the ground of the whole area; it says something about the place, not necessarily about one building. The median is the middle value: half sink faster, half slower.

Subsidence measured from space

European Union satellites measure the height of the ground. In Bernheze this covers 3,127 blocks of 100 by 100 metres. The typical (median) subsidence there is 0.3 mm per year (measured 2020–2025).

A satellite measures ground, streets and buildings together; for each block of 100 metres that is an average. A house on piles can stand still while the street in front of it sinks, and vice versa. Only where something reflects the radar signal well (roofs, roads) is there a measurement; not in meadows or on water. Source: Copernicus, the EU's Earth observation programme (Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, European Ground Motion Service (EGMS); bevat aangepaste Copernicus Sentinel-data).

According to the satellite measurements, the ground in Bernheze sinks by a median of 0.3 mm per year: about the same as the median for Noord-Brabant (0.5 mm per year). Of the 56 municipalities in Noord-Brabant with enough satellite measurements, Bernheze ranks 35, from fastest to slowest sinking.
