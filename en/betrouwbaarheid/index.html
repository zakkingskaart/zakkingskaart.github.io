How accurate is the satellite?

The map shows how satellites see the ground move, in blocks of 100 by 100 metres. We compare that with measurements taken by hand: the Rijkswaterstaat benchmarks across the Netherlands and the settlement bolts of the City of Amsterdam. This lets you see for yourself how well they agree. We do not change the data; we only put them side by side.

Compared with benchmarks in the Netherlands

We compared the satellite blocks with 12,349 benchmarks. For 70 out of 100 the difference is less than 1 mm per year, for 93 out of 100 less than 2 mm per year. Typically the satellite measures 0.5 mm per year more subsidence.

Why does the satellite often show faster sinking than the benchmarks? Benchmarks are in solid buildings and bridges. The satellite also measures streets, ground and meadows, which often sink more. They also measure over different years.

Compared with settlement bolts in Amsterdam

We compared the satellite blocks with 2,484 settlement bolts. For 89 out of 100 the difference is less than 1 mm per year, for 99 out of 100 less than 2 mm per year. Typically the satellite measures 0.3 mm per year more subsidence.

Settlement bolts are in the façades of ordinary houses and the city measures them often.

How is this calculated?

Included are benchmarks and settlement bolts with a recent rate that were measured at least until 2021.5, because the satellite measures over 2020–2025. For each benchmark or bolt we take the nearest satellite block within 75 metres; blocks with an uncertain measurement are not counted. ‘Median difference’ is the middle difference: a plus means the satellite measures more subsidence.

Zakkingskaart is a visualisation of existing public measurements, classified according to an existing standard (the KCAF guideline, table 3). No estimate per postcode, no judgement or advice and no foundation label. Independent initiative, separate from the municipality and Rijkswaterstaat; no rights can be derived from the data.
